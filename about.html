About us

Precision timing, from a new kind of chip company

Nexora Semiconductor Pty Ltd (Nexora Semi) is a fabless semiconductor startup developing precision timing ICs. We combine deep oscillator and mixed-signal design experience with AI-assisted engineering to develop products faster and at lower cost.

Our mission

Every connected, communicating or measuring device depends on an accurate clock. Nexora's mission is to deliver timing ICs with better performance-to-cost and power-to-performance ratios, built on a reusable mixed-signal platform that can serve many products and variants.

We develop our IP in South Australia, manufacture through established international foundry and packaging partners, and sell worldwide, building Australian capability in precision semiconductor design.

Frequently asked questions

What does Nexora Semi do?

We are developing precision timing ICs: TCXO ICs and PLL-based TCXOs first, then OCXO controller ICs, clock generators and jitter cleaners, on a reusable mixed-signal platform designed with an AI-assisted flow.

How does Nexora use AI in chip design?

AI agents operate chip-design tools by script. They generate schematics, build test benches and run corner and Monte Carlo simulations, generate layout placement, power and routing, run DRC, LVS and parasitic extraction, fix violations and write design reports. A senior designer reviews every stage.

Does AI replace the chip designer?

No. The designer sets the architecture and specifications and signs off each stage. The agents take on the time-consuming execution work that would otherwise need a team of analog, digital and layout engineers.

What has the flow achieved so far?

In October 2026, about 15 analog blocks were laid out by AI agents and verified DRC/LVS-clean in roughly two weeks, with one designer reviewing. A full automated layout and verification pass takes under a minute per block, and one AI-generated regulator layout was 17% smaller than the hand layout with about 6 dB better supply rejection. A complete chip has not yet been taped out with the flow.

What is the competitive advantage?

The flow targets designing a small mixed-signal ASIC in about one month with one senior designer, compared with roughly six months and a three-person team in a conventional flow. These are targets based on block-level results, and full-chip validation is our current milestone.

What happens next?

Full-chip integration (in progress), tape-out of our first precision-timing ASIC on a multi-project wafer shuttle, silicon characterisation, then engineering samples for customers and a growing team. See R&D progress.
